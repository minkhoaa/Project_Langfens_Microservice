ABSTRACT The shallow hydrothermal system of the Tagoro submarine volcano (Canary Islands, Spain) has been discharging low‐temperature, nutrient‐rich, acidic fluids high in CO 2 and H 2 S since March 2012. Despite influencing local microbial communities, the effects of these hydrothermal fluids on community structure and diversity across spatial gradients remain poorly understood. Using 16S rRNA gene sequencing, we observed significant differences in prokaryotic community structure from epipelagic waters to benthic habitats affected by hydrothermal activity. Prokaryotic richness and diversity decreased closer to hydrothermal sources, yet novel taxa were prevalent across all hydrothermally influenced habitats. Archaeal diversity was low and dominated by ammonia‐oxidising Nitrosopumilaceae , while bacterial communities were more diverse, including iron‐oxidising Mariprofundaceae , methane‐oxidising Methylomonadaceae and metabolically versatile Rhodobacteraceae . Molecular analyses revealed microbial specialisation within these families, complementing scanning electron microscopy observations of active bacterial biomineralization in chimney fragments linked to Mariprofundaceae members. Together, these findings offer new insights into the ecology and functional potential of microbial assemblages in the Tagoro hydrothermal system, suggesting their involvement in nitrogen, iron, sulphur and methane cycling. This study advances our understanding of microbial distribution patterns in shallow‐sea hydrothermal fields and highlights the high prevalence of novel taxa with potential biotechnological relevance. Hydrothermal activity at the Tagoro submarine volcano shapes distinct epipelagic and benthic prokaryotic communities. The simultaneous presence of light and geothermally reduced compounds makes these systems high‐energy environments where both photosynthesis and chemosynthesis take place (Tarasov et al. 2005 ; Price and Giovannelli 2017 ), contributing to enhance primary production in the surface layer (e.g., Buck et al. 2018 ; Bonnet et al. 2023 ; Tilliette et al. 2023 ; Pérez‐Barrancos et al. 2025 ). These unique environments harbour diverse microbial communities adapted to extreme conditions (Caramanna et al. 2021 ) and sustain the food web by promoting the accumulation and transformation of organic matter (Gomez‐Saez et al. 2017 ). Additionally, microorganisms often develop new habitats (e.g., microbial mats and biofilms) (Danovaro et al. 2024 ) and establish potential symbiotic relationships with biota colonising these newly formed ecosystems (Dubilier et al. 2008 ). While shallow‐water vents have been documented on several geological settings, they remain significantly less explored than their deep‐sea (&gt; 1000 mbsl) counterparts (Price and Giovannelli 2017 ). The 2011 submarine eruption that took place just 1.8 km off the coast of El Hierro (Canary Islands), provided a rare opportunity to investigate underwater volcanism and its subsequent shallow hydrothermal activity in a relatively accessible system. The eruption lasted 5 months and formed the Tagoro underwater volcano (Álvarez‐Valero et al. 2023 ; Vázquez et al. 2023 ). In March 2012, the system evolved into a diffuse degassing stage, with the affected area concentrated within 0.5 km of the main crater, extending from approximately 127 mbsl (main crater) to 88 mbsl (summit) (Santana‐Casiano et al. 2016 ; Martín‐Díaz et al. 2024 ). This has resulted in a 7600 m 2 shallow hydrothermal system covered with iron (Fe)‐oxide deposits (González et al. 2020 ) and characterised by thousands of vent orifices discharging low‐temperature (21°C–33.3°C) low‐pH (−1.37 average anomaly) fluids (Martín‐Díaz et al. 2024 ), together with CO 2 and reduced species degassing (Santana‐Casiano et al. 2013 , 2016 ). Vent structures include fissures, fractures and chimney‐like morphologies, often colonised by microbial mats (Danovaro et al. 2017 ; Sotomayor‐García et al. 2023 ; Martín‐Díaz et al. 2024 ). Diffuse hydrothermal emissions from Tagoro have significantly altered the physical and chemical properties of seawater and marine sediments since the eruptive phase (Santana‐Casiano et al. 2013 , 2016 ; Santana‐González et al. 2017 ; Fraile‐Nuez et al. 2018 ; González et al. 2020 ; González‐Vega et al. 2020 ). Hydrothermal discharge creates persistent anomalies including elevated temperature, and reduced salinity and density in the water column (Fraile‐Nuez et al. 2018 ). These emissions also produce marked ORP anomalies and decrease in pH, driven by the release of reduced, acidic fluids and CO 2 ‐rich gases (Santana‐Casiano et al. 2013 , 2016 ). Vertical water‐column profiles further revealed positive Fe 2+ concentration anomalies, coinciding with significant pH reductions, in waters near the main cone (Santana‐González et al. 2017 ).
